# AEROWING(aka GREENWING)
### Engineering a More Sustainable Future Through Aerospace & Mechanical Innovation

> **A student-led engineering initiative exploring sustainable aerospace technologies, aerodynamic design, propulsion, and practical engineering solutions for environmental and social impact.**

---

## Overview

**AeroWing** is an independent aerospace and mechanical engineering project focused on understanding, designing, and experimentally investigating engineering solutions that can contribute to a more sustainable future.

The project combines fundamental engineering principles with practical experimentation, CAD modelling, aerodynamic analysis, prototyping, and data-driven evaluation.

Rather than treating engineering as purely theoretical, GREENWING aims to establish a structured development process — from learning the underlying science to designing components, building prototypes, conducting controlled experiments, analysing results, and documenting improvements.

The long-term vision is to explore how aerospace and mechanical engineering can contribute to environmental sustainability and accessible technological innovation.

## Vision

To explore and develop practical engineering technologies that unite aerospace innovation, mechanical design, and environmental responsibility.

## Mission

- Apply fundamental engineering principles to real-world problems.
- Develop practical skills in aerodynamics, propulsion, mechanical design, and materials.
- Design, build, and test experimental prototypes.
- Use computational tools, CAD, and data analysis to support engineering decisions.
- Document the entire development process through transparent, reproducible technical records.
- Continuously improve designs through experimentation, critical analysis, and iterative development.

## Engineering Domains

| Domain | Focus |
|---|---|
| Aerodynamics | Airflow behaviour, lift, drag, pressure distribution, and flow visualisation |
| Mechanical Design | CAD modelling, component design, mechanisms, and prototyping |
| Propulsion | Fundamentals of thrust, energy conversion, and propulsion efficiency |
| Sustainability | Energy efficiency, material use, and environmentally responsible design |
| Experimental Engineering | Instrumentation, measurements, testing, and uncertainty analysis |
| Computational Engineering | Numerical methods, simulation fundamentals, and data visualisation |

## Project Objectives

1. Establish a strong foundation in mathematics, physics, and engineering fundamentals.
2. Learn to translate engineering concepts into CAD models and physical prototypes.
3. Develop experimental methods for observing and measuring aerodynamic behaviour.
4. Investigate the relationship between design parameters and engineering performance.
5. Compare theoretical predictions with experimental observations.
6. Build a documented portfolio demonstrating practical engineering ability.
7. Explore future research opportunities in sustainable aerospace and mechanical technologies.

## Engineering Methodology

AeroWing follows an iterative engineering workflow:

1. **Define** — Identify the problem, requirements, constraints, and success criteria.
2. **Research** — Study established theory and relevant technical literature.
3. **Design** — Develop concepts, CAD models, and engineering calculations.
4. **Build** — Manufacture or assemble a prototype where feasible.
5. **Test** — Conduct controlled experiments with appropriate safety measures.
6. **Analyse** — Evaluate results, measurement uncertainty, and model limitations.
7. **Improve** — Refine the design based on evidence.
8. **Document** — Publish findings, methods, limitations, and subsequent development steps.

## Safety & Engineering Responsibility

All physical experiments must be planned around appropriate risk assessments, protective equipment, and safe operating procedures.

Any future aerodynamic or propulsion experiments will be designed for controlled testing conditions. Potentially hazardous equipment, rotating machinery, high-speed airflow, electrical systems, and hot components must be handled with suitable safeguards and supervision.

Simulation results and preliminary calculations will not be presented as experimental validation.
